One of the biggest misconceptions about university is that a high GPA requires constant studying.

While academic performance matters for opportunities such as scholarships, exchange programmes, and postgraduate study, strong grades are often the result of effective planning and smart decision-making rather than endless hours of revision.
The following five habits can help.

## 1. Study the Assessment Before the Content

Before diving into lecture slides, review the assessment breakdown and marking rubric.

Understanding how a course is assessed helps you prioritise your effort. Some students spend hours preparing for examinations while overlooking coursework, participation, or presentations that could provide valuable marks.

The rubric is equally important. Rather than focusing only on completing an assignment, focus on what the rubric rewards. It often provides the clearest indication of what distinguishes a satisfactory submission from an excellent one.

## 2.Practise Questions Early

Many students wait until revision week before attempting past-paper questions. By then, it is often too late to identify and address weaknesses effectively.

Instead, start practising throughout the semester. A useful target is to complete at least one past-paper question for each course every week.

Regular practice helps identify weaknesses early, develop familiarity with examination-style questions, and build confidence well before the examination period begins.

Students can also make use of the (Library Examination Database)[https://www.library.ln.edu.hk/en/collection/digital/examdb].

## 3. Stay Ahead of Deadlines

One of the biggest challenges in university is managing multiple deadlines at the same time.

At the beginning of each semester, record all assignments, presentations, projects, quizzes, and examinations in a planner or Gantt chart. This helps identify busy periods early and avoid last-minute surprises.

Starting work ahead of time also creates opportunities to seek feedback, clarify uncertainties, and improve the quality of your work. Many students lose marks not because they lack ability, but because they begin too late.

## 4. Start Group Projects Early

Group projects often become stressful because planning starts too late.

As soon as groups are formed, establish a communication channel, agree on responsibilities, and set internal deadlines before the official submission date. Regular check-ins can help identify problems before they become serious.

Successful group projects are usually the result of clear expectations, early coordination, and consistent communication.

## 5. Use AI for Feedback

AI can be a valuable learning tool when used appropriately.

After completing a draft yourself, use AI to identify weaknesses, suggest improvements, or provide examples of stronger responses. Comparing your work with stronger examples can reveal areas for improvement and help you produce higher-quality submissions.

However, AI should be treated as a source of feedback rather than an authority. Lecturers, not AI, determine your grades, so assessment criteria and course requirements should always take priority.

Final Thoughts

A high GPA is not simply the result of studying longer hours. It is often the result of understanding how courses are assessed, preparing consistently, managing deadlines effectively, and seeking feedback early.

By working strategically rather than endlessly, you can achieve strong academic results while still making time for the experiences that make university memorable. A high GPA should be a by-product of working efficiently, not a result of sacrificing everything else university has to offer.
